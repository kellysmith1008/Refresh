# Project Retrospectives

Welcome to the contract retrospectives training. In this module, we'll provide the foundational knowledge you need to successfully conduct retrospectives on our projects. This training is geared toward project managers but relevant to all CivicActions employees. It provides the context for us to understand and embrace this initiative. This course will help you:

- Understand how retrospectives work
- Participate meaningfully regardless of role
- Translate insights into meaningful improvements

## What is a Retrospective?

A project retrospective is a structured, collaborative meeting held by a team to reflect on a completed project or work cycle (like a sprint) to analyze successes and failures, with the primary goal of identifying actionable improvements for future work.</br>
</br>
At CivicActions, **retrospectives are a core part of how we learn, improve, and collaborate**. We consider them more than just part of agile ceremonies; they are a structured way to:

* Reflect on what worked and what didn’t
* Identify improvements
* Turn insights into action

Retrospectives happen regularly because continuous improvement is essential to delivering quality work and adapting to change. 

## Why Retrospectives Matter**

Retrospectives are built on a simple idea:

> **Teams improve when they regularly reflect and adapt**.

--------

Retros help teams:

- Identify what worked well
- Surface challenges and blockers
- Improve collaboration and processes
- Create actionable next steps

They also support:

- Better decision-making
- Stronger team alignment
- Continuous learning and growth

--------

At CivicActions, retrospectives are also a space to:

- Celebrate wins
- Share feedback safely
- Improve without blame

## Who's Involved?

You join your first retrospective session. Who’s in the room?

- Project team members
- Project Manager or Scrum Master
- Product Owner
- Occasionally, observers like internal or external stakeholders, learners, or CORs

While several people and roles can be in the room during the retrospective, that doesn't mean everyone participates. Remember, a retrospective is the time to reflect on the sprint work or project cycle. Therefore, only those who contributed to the work should participate in the retrospective.

# **🛠️ Section 3: Making Retrospectives Effective**

During your next retrospective, the facilitator asks:

* What went well?
* What didn’t go well?
* What should we change?

These are common retrospective prompts.

Other formats include:

* Start / Stop / Continue
* Sad / Mad / Glad
* 4Ls (Loved, Learned, Lacked, Longed for) ([Atlassian][3])

---

# **📈 Section 4: From Insights to Continuous Improvement**

After the retrospective:

* Action items are documented
* Owners are assigned
* Progress is tracked across sprints

Teams often:

* Store action items in shared docs or tools
* Revisit them during sprint planning
* Track improvements over time ([guidebook.civicactions.com][2])
