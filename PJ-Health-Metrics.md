# Health Metrics  

Welcome to the health metrics training. In this module, you’ll learn what health metrics are, why they matter, and how to use them in your day-to-day work. This training is geared toward project managers but relevant to all CivicActions employees. It provides the context for us to understand and embrace this initiative. This information helps you:

- Explain the purpose of project health metrics
- Identify how project health is currently tracked and communicated
- Recognize baseline indicators of project health
- Understand how health metrics are reviewed and documented
- Apply this knowledge to your role at CivicActions

## What Are Health Metrics?

Health metrics help teams and leadership understand how a project is performing. At CivicActions, projects can be complex and fast-moving. Without clear signals, it’s hard to know:

- Are we on track?
- Are we staying within budget?
- Are there risks we need to address?

Health Metrics answer these questions by providing **consistent, visible indicators of project performance**. They aren't just numbers. Health metrics tell a story about the project’s overall health. They tell you what's working well and when something needs attention.

## Why Health Metrics Matter

Health Metrics are important because they:

- Help leadership make informed decisions  
- Surface risks early before they become bigger problems  
- Keep teams aligned on priorities  
- Improve transparency across projects  

Without clear metrics, teams may rely on assumptions or incomplete information. With health metrics, everyone works from a shared understanding of reality. This is especially important in distributed teams, where communication happens across tools and time zones.

### Example

If a project is behind schedule but no one flags it early, it can lead to:

- Missed deadlines  
- Budget overruns  
- Reduced client trust  

Health metrics help prevent this by making issues visible sooner.

## How Health Metrics Are Communicated

Project health information is shared across multiple channels. These include, but aren't limited to:

- **Project Slack core channels** 
  - Quick updates and team communication  
- **Financial reporting channels**
  - Budget and margin tracking  
- **Project status reports**
  - Structured summaries of progress  
- **Project planning artifacts**
  - Roadmaps, timelines, and deliverables  

Each channel serves a different purpose, but together they create a complete picture of project health. No single source tells the whole story. It's the combination of all sources that matters.

## Baseline Health Indicators

At CivicActions, we use a set of baseline indicators to assess project health. These indicators provide a high-level snapshot of project health.

These include:

1. **Budget and Margin**:
  - Are we staying within budget?  
- Is the project financially sustainable?

2. **On-Time Delivery**:
- Are we meeting deadlines and milestones?

3. **Customer Satisfaction**:
- Is the client happy with the work and communication?

> If any of these areas are off track, it may signal a need for deeper investigation.

------

_**Strong projects don’t just meet one indicator. They balance all three.**_

For example:
- A project delivered _on time_ but _over budget_ is still **at risk**.  
- A project _within budget_ but with _low customer satisfaction_ is also **at risk**.

## How Health Metrics Are Reviewed

Health metrics are collected and actively reviewed through:

- **Monthly reporting**:
  - Structured, recurring updates  
- **Leadership updates**:
  - High-level visibility and decision-making  
- **Internal team discussions**:
  - Day-to-day awareness and adjustments  

-------

This regular review ensures that:

- Issues are identified early  
- Teams can adapt quickly  
- Leadership stays informed  

Health metrics are most valuable when they lead to action. Tracking data without discussion or follow-up limits their impact.

## Evidence Artifacts

Health Metrics rely on evidence artifacts, which are documents and tools that support reporting. These artifacts provide the data behind the metrics. Let's take a look at some example artifacts:

| Artifact                  | Purpose                          |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Monthly status reports   | Financial and delivery reporting |
| Project plans/roadmaps   | Schedule visibility              |
| Budget documentation     | Financial tracking               |
| Staffing plans           | Resource planning                |

These artifacts may live in project drive folders or client systems, but they may live in other locations as well. Even though the exact location may vary, the goal is the same: **Keep information accessible and up-to-date**. 

If you're unsure where to find an artifact, ask your team or project lead. Knowing where information lives is part of maintaining project health. Following good documentation and governance practices ensures your information is always accessible and current.

## Summary

Health metrics aren't just about tracking project deliverables or client satisfaction. They are a key part of how we ensure project success. Proper tracking of and action on health metrics ensures:

- Visibility into project performance and risks  
- Baseline indicators like budget, delivery, and customer satisfaction are used for measurement 
- Metrics are communicated across multiple tools and channels  
- They are reviewed regularly to support decision-making  
- The metrics rely on artifacts to provide supporting evidence
